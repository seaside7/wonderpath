# WonderPath: website copy review and content writing rules

Version: 1.0 · 12 October 2026

## Purpose and scope

Use this document as the editorial reference for WonderPath's website and product copy. It includes the public website review and reusable instructions for a content-writing agent.

Reviewed: Indonesian landing page, seven expanded FAQ answers, public registration page, and public login page at https://wonderpath.itsmesaid.id on 12 October 2026. English landing-page copy, signed-in screens, validation errors, and backend functionality were not reviewed. Current text below is a snapshot, not proof that the described features work.

All replacements are proposals. Nothing has been published. “Verify” means a product fact must be confirmed before the replacement is used. Square-bracket placeholders must never appear in published copy.

## 1. Brand voice

Write like a parent explaining a useful learning tool to another parent: warm, clear, practical, and respectful. Let concrete product behavior carry the message.

The desired feeling is: “I understand what this does, how my child uses it, and what I can see as a parent.” Avoid copy that sounds like a motivational speech, a translated corporate brochure, or an AI assistant trying to sound cheerful.

### Audience and pronouns

| Context | Voice | Preferred address |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing page and parent onboarding | Warm, conversational, informative | kamu, anakmu |
| Parent reports | Specific and calm | child's name when available; otherwise anakmu |
| Child-facing instructions | Short, friendly, concrete | kamu; Atlas may use aku |
| Child feedback after an answer | Supportive without judgment | Belum tepat. Yuk, lihat penjelasannya. |
| Policy and account notices | Precise and neutral | clear nouns; avoid marketing language |
| Story narration | Appropriate to the reading level and story tone | character names and story perspective |

Prefer “anakmu” in parent marketing copy. Do not repeat it mechanically in every sentence. Use a name or omit the subject when the meaning stays clear. “Si Kecil” is not forbidden, but it should not be the default throughout the site.

### Language choices

- Use natural Bahasa Indonesia. Read each sentence aloud before accepting it.
- Conversational phrases such as “Kenalan sama Atlas” and “belajar bareng” can work. Do not force slang into every sentence.
- Prefer “tidak perlu,” “bisa,” “pilih,” “lihat,” and “dengarkan” over elaborate alternatives.
- Avoid unnecessary English terms in parent copy: use “pembuat WonderPath” instead of “founder,” “mengunduh” instead of “install,” and “jawaban benar” instead of “akurasi” where appropriate.
- Preserve official curriculum names, product names, names of people, and necessary technical terms.
- Use sentence case for headings and buttons. Preserve proper nouns: WonderPath, Atlas, Story Trail, Bahasa Indonesia, Cambridge.
- Use full stops and commas naturally. Avoid em dashes and decorative punctuation in marketing prose. Hyphens remain valid where grammar or identifiers require them.
- Use emoji sparingly and only when they help, such as an example reward. Do not use emoji to decorate every heading.

## 2. Mandatory writing rules

### Explain a behavior or benefit

Describe what the child or parent can actually do. Put the main point first. One sentence should normally carry one main idea.

| Avoid | Prefer | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teman belajar yang sabar, seru, dan nggak bikin minder. | Belajar bareng Atlas. | Removes generic adjectives and an unnecessary negative feeling. |
| Mulutnya ikut bergerak. | Anakmu bisa mendengarkan penjelasan Atlas. | Gives the reader a useful capability rather than an animation detail. |
| Tiga langkah simpel, satu jalan belajar. | Begini cara WonderPath bekerja. | Removes a forced brand metaphor. |
| Semua yang anak butuhkan untuk berkembang. | Latihan untuk anakmu. Ringkasan untuk kamu. | Describes scope without claiming completeness. |
| Pantau perkembangan cuma dalam 30 detik. | Lihat perkembangan belajar dalam satu ringkasan. | Avoids an untested time promise. |

If mouth animation is the subject of a technical spec or accessibility explanation, describe it there. For product marketing, let a demo show the animation.

### Avoid formulaic marketing

- Do not pad headings with three adjectives such as “sabar, seru, dan menyenangkan.”
- Avoid forced contrasts: “bukan sekadar X, melainkan Y,” “bukan cuma X, tapi Y,” and “ini bukan tentang X.” Use them only when a real distinction needs explaining.
- Avoid vague transformation claims: “membuka potensi,” “masa depan gemilang,” “perjalanan seunik dirinya,” and “solusi revolusioner.”
- Do not add an uplifting slogan after every paragraph.
- Do not change clear wording just to make it sound more impressive or different.
- Vary sentence length naturally. Short text is not automatically natural if every sentence has the same rhythm.
- Keep useful existing wording. Editing is not a requirement to rewrite every line.

### Respect children and parents

- Never label children “malas,” “tertinggal,” “lemah,” or “kurang pintar.” Describe the observed learning need.
- Do not imply that a child will become insecure or give up because of an ordinary difficulty.
- Do not shame parents with “kamu nggak tahu” or create guilt to encourage registration.
- Describe mistakes neutrally: “belum tepat,” “masih perlu latihan,” or “rumusnya masih tertukar.”
- Praise specific effort or action when supported. Do not infer effort from accuracy alone.
- Do not infer understanding from speed alone. Fast answers can be guesses; slower answers can reflect careful thinking.
- Avoid claims that WonderPath replaces a teacher, tutor, or parent unless the product is explicitly positioned and validated that way.

### Preserve truth and availability

- Never invent statistics, testimonials, success rates, learning outcomes, review methods, curriculum coverage, prices, trial duration, or feature behavior.
- Verify published question counts against available reviewed questions, not all generated database rows.
- Separate “available now,” “in beta,” and “planned.” Use future tense for unreleased features.
- Answer current grade availability before describing long-term ambitions.
- Do not suggest complete curriculum coverage when only certain grades, topics, or subjects are available.
- Explain Merdeka and Nasional as separate options only if the product has a meaningful confirmed distinction.
- “Soal diperiksa” must reflect an actual review process. It does not imply every story or all content is reviewed.
- Avoid absolute promises: “aman,” “selamanya,” “semua,” “pasti,” “langsung,” and “cukup” when they overstate evidence.
- Keep trial and beta pricing consistent across hero, signup, FAQ, and campaign copy.
- Use real contacts. Remove placeholders before inviting families.
- Do not write fictional personal experiences as if they belong to the founders.
- Do not invent a feature while making a rewrite more specific. If a replacement mentions a capability not established in the brief, mark it Verify.

### Describe Atlas accurately

Atlas is the learning mascot and guide. Light character language such as “Halo! Aku Atlas” or “Atlas ikut senang” can work if reflected in the experience.

Do not claim Atlas knows a child's feelings, fully understands the child, or has confirmed mastery after a few answers. Prefer “jawaban awal membantu Atlas mengenali kemampuan” over “Atlas tahu semua yang sudah dikuasai.” Distinguish observations from conclusions.

### Interface copy

- Start action buttons with a clear verb: Buat akun, Masuk, Pilih topik, Dengarkan, Simpan.
- Keep button text short, normally two to four words. Accuracy is more important than a rigid word count.
- Distinguish reading levels from school grades: “Level bacaan 2” versus “Kelas 2.”
- Use the same term for the same feature across pages.
- Keep the selected language through landing page, registration, login, onboarding, and errors.
- Label example dashboards and fictional profiles as examples.
- Use numeric labels with units: “1.240 poin,” “4 dari 7 hari,” and “81% jawaban benar.”
- Give errors a clear problem and next action. Do not invent a retry or recovery option that does not exist.

## 3. Content-agent workflow

This workflow is an editorial process; it does not require separate agents or a complex architecture.

1. Read the actual page, source text, target audience, screen purpose, and confirmed product facts.
2. Identify whether the copy is for parents, children, a report, a policy, or a story.
3. Extract claims that need confirmation. Check available authoritative product documentation or code before asking the owner. Do not guess.
4. Keep accurate, natural sentences. Rewrite awkward wording while preserving meaning, names, numbers, conditions, and disclosures.
5. Review for tone, repetition, judgmental language, unsupported claims, and terminology consistency.
6. Check the surrounding UI: does the button do what it says, is a feature live, and is example content labeled?
7. Return the proposed copy and any unresolved factual questions. Do not publish placeholders.

For a review, use a table with: Section, Current copy, Issue, Suggested copy, Verification needed. For a net-new draft, return the complete draft plus a brief list of material assumptions. Ask a focused question only when an unresolved fact materially changes the copy; proceed with independent sections in the meantime.

### Ready-to-paste instruction for OpenCode or a content agent

```text
Act as WonderPath's content writer and editor. Follow WonderPath_Content_Guide.md.

Write warm, direct Bahasa Indonesia, like a parent explaining the product to another parent. Use kamu and anakmu for parent copy without repeating them in every sentence. Use short, concrete language for children; Atlas may speak as aku.

Explain real product behavior. Avoid generic emotional slogans, stacks of three adjectives, forced brand metaphors, exaggerated claims, guilt, judgmental labels, and unnecessary implementation details. Use clear action labels and consistent terminology. Keep existing text when it already works.

Never invent product facts, features, curriculum coverage, prices, trial terms, reviewed-content counts, testimonials, or learning outcomes. Distinguish current availability, beta features, and plans. Label fictional examples. Verify facts from project documentation or implementation; if a material fact remains unclear, ask a focused question before finalizing that section. Never publish placeholders.

Preserve names, numbers, conditions, and factual meaning during edits. Do not infer mastery or carefulness from response speed alone. Do not claim Atlas knows the child's feelings.

Use the selected language consistently across the whole flow. For reviews, provide Current copy / Issue / Suggested copy / Verification needed. For drafts, provide ready-to-use copy and material assumptions. Read the result aloud and do one final pass for awkward phrasing and repetition.

These rules govern wording. They do not authorize deploying changes, publishing content, or changing product behavior.
```

## 4. Review checklist

- [ ] Is the audience clear?
- [ ] Does the reader understand the feature without knowing the implementation?
- [ ] Would a parent naturally say this sentence aloud?
- [ ] Have repeated “Si Kecil,” “anakmu,” “perjalanan,” and “seru” been reduced?
- [ ] Does every adjective add meaning?
- [ ] Does the copy avoid shaming or labeling children and parents?
- [ ] Are product claims backed by current facts?
- [ ] Are current availability and future plans clearly separated?
- [ ] Do prices, beta terms, grades, subjects, and curricula agree across pages?
- [ ] Are examples labeled, contacts real, and placeholders removed?
- [ ] Does every action label match what the UI does?
- [ ] Is the selected language preserved through the flow?
- [ ] Are unresolved questions marked for review rather than silently invented?

## 6. Additional rules for Story Trail

These supplement the website-copy rules; story narration should still have its own age-appropriate voice.

- Define reading level, language, genre, tone, characters, setting, and learning purpose before drafting.
- Create an outline before page text: character goal, obstacle, action, consequence, and resolution.
- Fantasy is allowed. Events must follow the rules of the story world.
- An unusual detail should develop character, setting, humor, or plot. Do not add random absurdity solely to make a story feel creative.
- Match humor to the approved tone. A dragon eating nasi goreng can fit a deliberately silly story, but may feel misplaced in a gentle adventure.
- Keep names, appearance, abilities, locations, objects, chronology, and illustration descriptions consistent across pages.
- Use vocabulary and sentence complexity appropriate to the defined reading level. Do not equate reading level with school grade without an established mapping.
- For biographies and factual stories, separate invented dialogue or scenes from verified facts. Do not fabricate historical quotations or events.
- Write comprehension questions answerable from the actual story.
- Review the entire story for coherence, natural Bahasa Indonesia, repetition, and age appropriateness before generating illustrations or narration.
